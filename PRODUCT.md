# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Restaurant owners and their staff (floor managers, receptionists). They use Zambiq daily, typically on a laptop or tablet from the kitchen, front-of-house, or back office. Primary context: pre-service prep and mid-service triage — checking arrivals, updating reservation statuses, coordinating shifts, and tracking delivery orders alongside walk-in traffic.

## Product Purpose
Zambiq is a modular management platform for local entrepreneurs, starting with restaurants. It replaces fragmented workflows (WhatsApp, phone calls, Excel sheets, paper notepads) with one calm workspace. Module 1: reservations. Subsequent modules: staff planning, delivery integrations, analytics, accounting, and more. Success means an owner opens Zambiq each morning and immediately understands the full day — bookings, team, delivery orders — without switching between tools.

## Brand Personality
Calm · Clear · Confident. The tone is a capable, unhurried colleague: direct without being cold, professional without being corporate. Copy should feel like a well-organised local operator, not a Silicon Valley startup.

## Anti-references
Not generic enterprise SaaS: no Salesforce/HubSpot-style data-dense dashboards, no corporate coldness, no feature-bloat UIs built for Fortune 500 ops teams. The product is for neighbourhood businesses and must feel human-scaled and purposeful.

## Design Principles
1. **Calm over clever** — every screen should lower the operator's heart rate, not raise it. Reduce noise before adding features.
2. **Clarity at a glance** — today's reservations, incoming orders, and who's working must be readable in 5 seconds without hunting.
3. **Local but polished** — warm materials and considered weight without feeling corporate or over-designed. Confidence through restraint.
4. **Trust through reliability** — interactions feel certain. Confirmations are immediate. Errors are actionable, never vague.
5. **Room to grow** — the dashboard gains modules over time. Design patterns must scale gracefully without turning messy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA baseline. Primary audience: full-sighted operators on modern desktop/tablet browsers. Priority areas: contrast on dark surface components (restaurant identity cards, stat cards with gradient backgrounds), legible form labels, and keyboard-accessible data tables.
